LAB THESIS / 02

Make discovery part of everyday computing.

A simulation is a world you can experiment with: change a force, a material, or a rule, then observe the consequences. Our vision is to bring that power to the Apple computers people already own. The Numi Suite studies bodies, minds, molecules, and artificial life; the Numi Lab Codex plugin connects a question in words to the local research tools.

THE QUESTIONS THAT DRIVE US

Small questions.
Whole new worlds.

Start with something you can picture. Follow it into the physics, intelligence, and life we’re still trying to understand.

  1. 01

    How does a body learn to balance?

    Can a learning system stay in control when the ground shifts, contact changes, or its actions arrive late?

    Why it matters A validated human simulation could help researchers test rehabilitation, prosthetics, and surgical ideas before they reach a patient. At scale, better tools could help protect millions of lives; demonstrating that benefit requires clinical evidence.

    Explore bodies and embodied learning
    Body & mind
  2. 02

    When can we trust a simulated world?

    A bag stretches. Fruit falls. What must the model preserve so a convincing animation also becomes a useful experiment?

    Why it matters Reliable contact and material models could help engineers design robots that handle fragile objects and work more safely around people, and test protective equipment before building it.

    Explore physics and its measured limits
    Physical fidelity
  3. 03

    How does matter become life?

    How do molecular interactions build stable structures—and which mechanisms let artificial organisms adapt, repair, and reproduce?

    Why it matters Validated molecular models could help scientists narrow down drug candidates and investigate disease mechanisms before costly laboratory studies. Artificial-life experiments could also reveal ideas for systems that repair themselves.

    Explore molecules and living systems
    Life across scales
  4. 04

    Can a world surprise its creator?

    How do we recognize something new without deciding in advance what discovery must look like?

    Why it matters Worlds that produce unexpected behavior could help researchers investigate adaptation and resilience—and inspire new ways to build machines that cope with changing conditions.

    Explore artificial life
    Open-ended discovery
  5. 05

    Could anyone run an experiment on a Mac?

    Can natural-language guidance and Apple-native computation make simulation accessible while keeping assumptions, checks, and results visible?

    Why it matters A student could investigate balance, a designer could explore a softer robotic grip, and a small research team could test an idea locally. The ambition is to make experimentation available far beyond specialist labs.

A doorway from Codex into Numi Lab.

The plugin teaches Codex to discover your installed Numi runtime and its available commands. Codex can then help work with local experiments, training, and evaluation through the same native tools used by the lab.

  1. AskDescribe what you want to investigate.
  2. ExploreLet Codex discover the runtime and help choose an experiment.
  3. InspectReview what ran, what was measured, and what remains uncertain.
Set up the plugin on an Apple silicon Mac

From a Numi Lab checkout with the runtime prerequisites installed:

./tools/numi doctor
./tools/numi codex install

The installer registers the repository marketplace and enables the Numi Lab plugin. Start a new Codex task after installation. Building the native runtime requires the Xcode/Metal toolchain, CMake 3.28+, Ninja, SQLite3, and LibXml2.

Read the plugin and setup guide

Today: source-based research tools with explicit requirements and measured limits. Our ambition: a simpler path from an everyday question to a reproducible local experiment. Availability and performance depend on the installed capability and your Mac.

NUMI SOLVER / STANDALONE ELASTIC VOLUME

81,920 tetrahedra.
Impact and recovery.

A volume with 14,993 shared nodes falls onto a frictionless plane on the M4 Pro. Its mass center falls from 85.07 to 47.46 mm, then recovers to 74.68 mm over 0.5 simulated seconds. The native replay matches exactly; the half-timestep run differs by at most 93.11 µm. All 101 captured states in each run pass independent geometry and mass checks. The independently audited numerical energy bounds are 0.254% and 0.122% of initial energy, within the authored 1% budget. A new matched 25 µs run at 10,240 tetrahedra also passes its captured-state audits, but the two resolutions differ by up to 9.34 mm at matched nodes against the 1 mm target. Spatial convergence fails.

Read the native volume result ↗

The video renders all 101 exact exported native boundary states without smoothing or posed deformation. Source, binary, audit and media fingerprints · Matched-resolution audit. This standalone volume has no fruit or bag. Separate bounded CPU studies reproduce a shared yarn/elastic-body contact step and a preventive face-area candidate; neither repairs the full bag scene. Measured material, reciprocal cloth/fruit coupling and complete physical work closure remain open. Earlier 10,240-tetrahedron video.
